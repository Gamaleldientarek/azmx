# Sub-Brand Voices: Colab, Majarah, Clix, Anatomi

How the four brands under AZM X sound. Read this whenever the deliverable carries a sub-brand name rather than AZM X. Source: the AZM X Unified Communication Strategy tone-of-voice appendix.

**This file is voice only. It carries no visual system.** Every palette, logo, font, and layout rule in this skill is AZM X's. There is no Colab, Majarah, Clix, or Anatomi visual system here. If asked for a sub-brand deliverable, apply that brand's voice and **ask the user which visual system to use**. Never silently dress a Colab or Majarah piece in AZM X navy and Electric. Colab has its own installed skill, `colab-design` — use it for anything Colab-visual.

AZM X's own voice and Four Dimensions live in `voice-and-tone.md`, not here.

## What all five share

One ecosystem, five voices. Sub-brand copy sounds different from AZM X copy, and each brand's copy sounds different from its siblings. The common thread is strategic thinking backed by evidence. The house mechanics in `voice-and-tone.md` — no AI tells, no em-dash defaults, no empty intensifiers, max 3 hashtags, no emojis in copy — apply to all five without exception.

| Brand | Archetype | Formality | Technicality | Attitude | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colab | The Empathetic Analyst | Adaptable | Translates complexity | Confident in the evidence | Balances vision and action |
| Majarah | The Inclusive Mentor | Slightly informal | Accessible and clear | Bold and encouraging | Balanced |
| Clix | The Trusted Operator | Mid-formal | Radically simple | Quietly confident | Pragmatic and scalable |
| Anatomi | The Analyst–Curator | Mid-formal | Balanced | Neutral and insightful | Pragmatic and aspirational |

The calibration examples in each section are **verbatim from the deck**. They are the tuning samples. Do not paraphrase them, do not modernise them, do not swap them between brands.

## Colab — The Empathetic Analyst

The evidence engine for clients. Precision of a data scientist, clarity of a good storyteller. Curious, sharp, focused on uncovering what is actually true so clients can decide with less risk. Formality moves with context: formal and precise in reports, conversational in workshops. Rigorous going into the data, simple coming out. The voice exists to turn evidence into action. Positioning: the leading UX lab in the region.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Adaptable | Authoritative · Precise · Evidence-based · Collaborative | Stiff · Vague · Overly casual · Jargony |
| Technicality | We translate complexity | Rigorous · Data-driven · Simplified · Actionable | Simplistic · Superficial · Confusing · Abstract |
| Attitude | Confident in our evidence | Bold in insights · Neutral in facts · Curious · Empathetic | Arrogant · Biased · Subjective · Dismissive |
| Purpose | Balances vision and action | Evidence-driven presenting findings · Action-oriented giving recommendations | — |

**Calibration examples** (verbatim):

| Dimension | Wrong, one direction | Wrong, the other direction | Colab voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *Too formal.* "The aforementioned findings necessitate the immediate prioritization of the navigational architecture." | *Too informal.* "So yeah, the nav is a mess, we should probably fix it soon." | "Our evidence shows that redesigning the navigation is the highest-impact priority for improving the user experience." |
| Technicality | *Too technical.* "Our biometric analysis showed a 15% increase in galvanic skin response correlating with task incompletion." | *Too simple.* "Users got upset when they couldn't finish." | "During the checkout process, we measured a significant spike in user frustration, which directly correlates with task abandonment." |
| Attitude | *Too subjective.* "Your users absolutely hate the new feature." | *Too neutral.* "Some users expressed negative feedback regarding the new feature." | "The data shows that 85% of users who interacted with the new feature abandoned the task. Our insight is that it fails to meet their primary expectation." |

**Purpose modes:**

| Mode | Surfaces | Goal | Example (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence-Driven | Research reports, audits, analytics dashboards | Be the objective source of truth. Trust comes from rigour and accuracy. | "Our analysis of the user journey revealed a 40% drop-off rate at the payment stage, with 72% of those users failing to complete the task" |
| Action-Oriented | Executive summaries, workshops, project plans | Give clear, confident, business-focused recommendations that enable a decision. | "Based on this evidence, our primary recommendation is to redesign the payment flow in Q1 to reduce friction and capture this lost revenue" |

## Majarah — The Inclusive Mentor

The community catalyst. Warmth of a host, insight of an industry pioneer. Generous with knowledge, open to questions, aimed at sparking the next idea. Friendly and human without being sloppy; welcoming to students and experts in the same sentence. Knowledge is shared, never gatekept. Positioning: the region's go-to UX gathering.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Slightly informal | Friendly · Human · Welcoming · Inclusive | Sloppy · Overly casual · Stiff · Elitist |
| Technicality | Accessible and clear | Simple · Clear · Accessible · Understandable | Jargony · Superficial · Simplistic · Vague |
| Attitude | Bold and encouraging | Bold · Encouraging · Vision-driven · Supportive | Neutral · Timid · Trend-chasing · Prescriptive |
| Purpose | Balanced | Inspirational sharing vision · Pragmatic sharing knowledge | — |

**Calibration examples** (verbatim):

| Dimension | Wrong, one direction | Wrong, the other direction | Majarah voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *Too formal.* "All attendees are hereby invited to the post-conference networking symposium." | *Too informal.* "Yo, let's hang out after the talks." | "Ready to connect? Join us for the after-party to meet the speakers and fellow creators." |
| Technicality | *Too technical.* "This session will deconstruct the semiotics of asynchronous digital affordances." | *Too simple.* "We'll talk about buttons and stuff." | "This session explores how we create intuitive digital cues for users in today's apps." |
| Attitude | *Too neutral.* "There are many ways to approach user research." | *Too prescriptive.* "You must follow this exact research method." | "Let's explore a bolder approach to user research that could unlock deeper insights." |

Two notes on that Attitude row. The deck has its two labels swapped: it tags "There are many ways to approach user research" as *too arrogant*, which it is not — it is the timid, neutral failure Majarah's profile warns against, and the prescriptive line is the other pole. The labels above are corrected; the samples are untouched. Second, the Majarah voice column uses *unlock*, a word `voice-and-tone.md` bans. Keep the example verbatim as a calibration sample; do not reuse that word in new Majarah copy.

## Clix — The Trusted Operator

The smart, approachable ERP. Calm confidence of a detail-oriented expert who cares about accuracy, efficiency, and a business running smoothly. An everyday partner, so approachable; a system of record for finance, so never sloppy. Plain business language, no enterprise buzzwords: features are explained by what they do for the user this week. Positioning: the trusted ERP alternative for SMEs — simple, reliable, future-ready.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |

| Formality | Mid-formal | Professional · Friendly · Clear · Trustworthy | Sloppy · Overly casual · Stiff · Robotic |
| Technicality | Radically simple | Simple · Jargon-free · Accessible · Human-centric | Overly technical · Simplistic · Vague · Confusing |
| Attitude | Quietly confident | Confident in accuracy · Bold in simplicity · Reliable · Calm | Loud · Hype-driven · Timid · Uncertain |
| Purpose | Pragmatic and scalable | Pragmatic on today's needs · Scalable on the future | — |

## Anatomi — The Analyst–Curator

The region's reference point for digital product design. Methodical precision of an analyst, clarity of a curator. Observes patterns, organises them, explains what they mean for design in the region. Authority comes from objectivity, so the default is neutral and the evidence carries the argument. Bold only when pointing at an industry-wide gap or opportunity. Technical in evaluation reports, simple in pattern showcases.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Mid-formal | Authoritative · Professional · Accessible · Clear | Stiff · Academic · Overly casual · Robotic |
| Technicality | Balanced | Technical in reports · Simple in showcases · Precise · Jargon-free | Obscure · Overly simplistic · Vague · Inconsistent |
| Attitude | Neutral and insightful | Neutral · Objective · Evidence-based · Insightful | Opinionated · Subjective · Hype-driven · Judgmental |
| Purpose | Pragmatic and aspirational | Pragmatic providing tools and data · Aspirational discussing industry growth | — |

**Calibration examples** (verbatim):

| Dimension | Wrong, one direction | Wrong, the other direction | Anatomi voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *Too formal.* "The aforementioned heuristics evaluation indicates a suboptimal deviation from established usability paradigms." | *Too informal.* "So, we checked out the app and the signup flow is kinda confusing." | "Our evaluation shows that the sign-up process does not follow key usability principles, creating friction for new users." |
| Technicality | *Too technical.* "Note the implementation of a floating action button with a z-index of 5." | *Too simple.* "The button is on top." | "This app uses a prominent floating action button to keep the primary action always accessible." |

## The one trap per brand

Each voice fails in a predictable direction. Watch for its own failure mode first.

| Brand | Most likely failure | The correction |
|---|---|---|
| Colab | Presenting a finding as an opinion, or an opinion as a finding. | Name the number, then name the insight drawn from it. Keep the two sentences separate. |
| Majarah | Warmth sliding into looseness, or inclusivity sliding into vagueness. | Friendly is not casual. Keep the invitation specific: who, what, when, why come. |
| Clix | Reaching for enterprise vocabulary to sound credible, or hype to sound exciting. | Say what the feature does for the user this week, in the words a small-business owner uses. |
| Anatomi | Ranking things by taste rather than by method. | Every judgement cites the heuristic, the score, or the comparison set. No superlatives without evidence. |

## Language

Sub-brand copy is typically English, with Arabic for market-facing work. Majarah, being community-facing in Saudi, carries the most Arabic. When translating, match the brand's archetype rather than the English sentence: Colab stays precise, Majarah stays warm, Clix stays plain, Anatomi stays neutral. Translation guidance lives in `content-prompts.md`.

## Picking a voice

1. Identify the brand on the deliverable, not the team producing it. A Colab case study written by AZM X's team is Colab voice.
2. Identify the surface, which sets the Purpose mode. Reports and support docs take the pragmatic mode; keynotes and homepages take the inspirational or aspirational mode.
3. Read that brand's three calibration rows aloud before writing. They calibrate faster than any rule.
4. AZM X copy about a sub-brand is AZM X voice. Sub-brand copy about itself is the sub-brand's voice. Parent-house announcements naming several brands are AZM X voice.
5. Ask which visual system applies. This file does not answer that question.
