# Voice

**Majarah's voice is owned by the `azmx-brand` skill, not this one.** This file is the short version plus the pointer, so a visual task does not need to load the whole communication strategy.

For anything substantial — writing posts, articles, invitations, session descriptions, English-to-Arabic localisation — load `azmx-brand` and read its `references/sub-brand-voices.md`. The calibration examples there are verbatim from the AZM X Unified Communication Strategy and must not be paraphrased.

## Archetype: The Inclusive Mentor

The community catalyst. Warmth of a host, insight of an industry pioneer. Generous with knowledge, open to questions, aimed at sparking the next idea. Friendly and human without being sloppy; welcoming to students and experts in the same sentence. **Knowledge is shared, never gatekept.**

Positioning: **the region's go-to UX gathering.** Audience motion: primarily B2C.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Slightly informal | Friendly · Human · Welcoming · Inclusive | Sloppy · Overly casual · Stiff · Elitist |
| Technicality | Accessible and clear | Simple · Clear · Accessible · Understandable | Jargony · Superficial · Simplistic · Vague |
| Attitude | Bold and encouraging | Bold · Encouraging · Vision-driven · Supportive | Neutral · Timid · Trend-chasing · Prescriptive |
| Purpose | Balanced | Inspirational sharing vision · Pragmatic sharing knowledge | — |

## Calibration (verbatim — do not paraphrase)

| Dimension | Wrong, one direction | Wrong, the other | Majarah voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *Too formal.* "All attendees are hereby invited to the post-conference networking symposium." | *Too informal.* "Yo, let's hang out after the talks." | "Ready to connect? Join us for the after-party to meet the speakers and fellow creators." |
| Technicality | *Too technical.* "This session will deconstruct the semiotics of asynchronous digital affordances." | *Too simple.* "We'll talk about buttons and stuff." | "This session explores how we create intuitive digital cues for users in today's apps." |
| Attitude | *Too neutral.* "There are many ways to approach user research." | *Too prescriptive.* "You must follow this exact research method." | "Let's explore a bolder approach to user research that could unlock deeper insights." |

## House mechanics — apply without exception

These come from `azmx-brand` and override the strategy deck wherever they conflict:

- **Three hashtags maximum**, at the end. The deck permits 3–5 (5–10 on Instagram); it is superseded.
- **No emojis in copy.** The deck encourages them; it is superseded.
- **No mandatory CTA.** The deck mandates one per post; it is superseded. Include a CTA only when a genuine next step exists.
- **No AI tells.** Banned: *empower, unlock, elevate, seamlessly, leverage, robust*. No em-dash-as-default.
- **Never invent a message.** Personas carry verbatim core messages in `azmx-brand`'s own `audiences-and-messaging.md`. Quote or tighten; never soften, never move a message between brands.

## Where voice meets this design system

Two places the visual system constrains the copy, worth knowing when you write for a slide:

**The two-tone headline needs a meaningful split.** The accent line is where the emphasis lands, so the second half must carry the payload — `A COMMUNITY` / **`IN ORBIT.`**, `THREE FOUNDATIONS.` / **`ONE COMMUNITY.`** Write headlines as two-part phrases where the second part is the point. A headline that splits into `THE PLATFORM HAS` / `THREE PILLARS` puts the emphasis on the wrong half.

**Eyebrows are two to four words, caps, no punctuation.** `SECTION 01 — ABOUT`, `PHASE 02`, `BE PART OF THE GALAXY`. They orient; they do not inform.

**Stat labels are one or two words.** `MEETUPS`, `ATTENDEES`, `DESIGNERS REACHED`. The numeral carries the message.

## A standing content warning

The v1 deck contains **five named testimonials and two statistics that appear in no source document** — see `decision-log.md` D-11. Named quotes invented for a client deck are a real exposure.

When writing Majarah social proof: source it from the attendee video, the community itself, or leave the slot empty. Majarah's whole positioning is that it is a real community with real people in it — inventing the people is the one thing that undermines the brand rather than expressing it.
