# AZMX Voice & Tone

How AZMX sounds, in English and Arabic. The visual system's ethos applies to words too: restraint is the luxury. Say less, mean more.

This file governs AZM X, the parent house. Colab, Majarah, Clix, and Anatomi each have a different voice: see `sub-brand-voices.md`. The mechanics in this file apply to all five, with one narrow carve-out: the emoji ban is a rule about copy, and the email design system's section-header and digest chip glyph is exempt (see the emoji row below).

## The voice in one line

Confident, editorial, human, premium. Never templated, never corporate-stiff, never AI-generated in feel.

The strategy deck's framing, which says the same thing from the outside: AZM X's voice is that of a **Visionary Expert and Strategic Partner**, a pioneering guide and a trusted advisor. Authoritative but accessible, bold but respectful, visionary in outlook and practical in approach.

## Brand language

- **Tagline territory:** Forward · Human Always · Moving Ideas Forward.
- **Philosophy line (client-locked, 2026-07-24):** Designing the Future of Experience.
- **Boilerplate:** AZMX is a leading Saudi digital consultancy. Never "studio", "agency", or "boutique".
- **Bilingual brand:** copy is typically English, Arabic appears in logo lockups, emails, and market-facing work. Internal communication is Arabic-first. Azm X carries both scripts. In Arabic emails, kashida-stretched wordmarks are copy-pasted, never retyped.

## Tone rules

1. **Confident, not boastful.** State what AZMX does plainly. Let the work carry the weight; skip superlatives like "world-class" and "cutting-edge."
2. **Editorial, not marketing-speak.** Write like a premium magazine, not a landing page. Short declarative sentences. Concrete nouns over abstractions.
3. **Human, always.** Warm and direct. Address the reader as a person. Contractions are fine in English. No jargon walls.
4. **Premium means quiet.** No exclamation marks doing the selling. No ALL-CAPS urgency. Scarcity of emphasis is what makes emphasis land, exactly like Electric blue on the page.

## The Four Dimensions

The four axes AZM X's voice is tuned on. Check a draft against all four before it ships.

| Dimension | Position | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formality** | Mid-Formal. Authoritative but accessible; expertise without stiffness. | Authoritative · Polished · Professional · Respectful | Stiff · Academic · Overly casual · Jargony |
| **Technicality** | Balanced. Expertise is worth nothing unheard. Simplify the complex without becoming simplistic. | Expert · Knowledgeable · Clear and precise · Educational | Simplistic · Patronising · Obscure · Overly technical |
| **Attitude** | Bold but Respectful. Challenge the status quo with the integrity of a trusted partner. | Confident · Pioneering · Bold and visionary · Thoughtfully provocative | Arrogant · Abrasive · Reckless · Dismissive |
| **Purpose** | Context-Dependent. Inspirational when sharing the vision, pragmatic when discussing delivery. | See the Purpose table below | — |

**Calibration examples** (verbatim from the deck — these are the tuning samples, do not paraphrase them):

| Dimension | Wrong, one direction | Wrong, the other direction | AZM X voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *Too formal.* "The aforementioned framework necessitates the utilization of agile methodologies to ensure optimal project velocity." | *Too informal.* "Basically, we gotta use agile to make stuff go faster." | "We use an agile framework to ensure our projects maintain momentum and deliver value efficiently." |
| Technicality | *Too technical.* "Our containerized microservices architecture leverages a Kubernetes orchestrator for dynamic scaling." | *Too simple.* "We make your website work well with lots of users." | "We build resilient, scalable systems that can grow with your business, ensuring reliability and performance under pressure." |
| Attitude | *Too arrogant.* "Our way is the only way to succeed in this market." | *Too neutral.* "There are several options available for market entry." | "We believe a human-centric approach is the most powerful and sustainable path to market leadership in the region." |

**Purpose modes:**

| Mode | Surfaces | Goal | Example (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inspirational** — sharing the vision | Homepage, keynotes, thought leadership | Paint the future and inspire confidence in AZM X's leadership. | "We are not just building digital products; we are elevating the digital maturity of the entire region, in line with Vision 2030" |
| **Pragmatic** — discussing delivery | Proposals, SOWs, project reports | Be clear and direct about tangible outcomes, efficiency, and measurable ROI. | "This SOW outlines a three-phase project, with bi-weekly check-ins to ensure full alignment and on-time delivery of all milestones" |

The Inspirational example uses *elevating*, a word the mechanics below ban. It stays verbatim because it is a calibration sample. Do not carry that word into new copy.

## Writing mechanics (the no-AI-tells rules)

Copy must read as written by a human, and an elite one. These are instant AI tells; never ship them:

- Em-dashes as the default connector. Use commas, periods, or a colon instead.
- Triads everywhere ("fast, simple, and powerful"). One strong claim beats three padded ones.
- Empty intensifiers: "truly," "seamlessly," "effortlessly," "robust," "leverage," "elevate," "unlock," "empower," "delve."
- Symmetrical paragraph lengths and mirrored sentence openers. Vary rhythm like a human editor would.
- Every sentence hedged ("can help," "may enable"). Commit: it does or it does not.
- Summaries that restate the heading. If the heading said it, the body adds something new or gets cut.

The strategy deck's own prose is saturated with these tells. Its verbatim core messages and calibration examples are quoted as-is throughout the reference files, but its vocabulary never enters new AZMX copy.

## Universal writing principles

Five principles for every piece of copy, from a tweet to a proposal.

| Principle | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Lead with value** | Answer "what's in it for me?" with a clear benefit: useful information, an insight, or a story worth the reader's minute. |
| **Be clear and concise** | Short sentences, no jargon, the point up front. Write conversationally, as if speaking to one person. |
| **Write like a human** | Approachable and authentic. Use contractions. Read it aloud; if it sounds like a robot, rewrite it. The target reaction is "you know me". |
| **Have a clear purpose** | Every piece carries one takeaway message and one job. **AZMX does not mandate a CTA** (see below); it does mandate knowing what the piece is for. |
| **Use hashtags strategically** | Three maximum, at the end. Mix broad, niche, and branded tags within that three. |

## By format

- **Headlines and heroes:** serif voice, short, mixed-case. A single sharp idea. No period at the end.
- **Eyebrows/labels:** UPPERCASE, 1 to 3 words, functional not clever.
- **Body copy:** sentence case, 22px voice on slides, short paragraphs. Front-load the point.
- **Stats:** let the number talk. Label in plain words underneath, no adjectives.
- **Quotes/testimonials:** verbatim, trimmed only for length, attributed with name and title.
- **Emails:** subject lines under 45 characters, preheader completes the subject rather than repeating it. Arabic emails follow the RTL rules in the email design system.
- **Social/LinkedIn:** first line earns the click alone (it is all people see collapsed). No hashtag walls; 3 maximum, at the end.

### Platform voice

Universal principles always apply; tone adapts to context.

| Platform | Voice | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Authoritative, professional, insightful | Share expertise and start a professional discussion. Structure for readability: white space, bullets, or a numbered list for longer thoughts. Mid-formal, expert but accessible. |
| **Instagram** | Creative, personable, inspiring | Tell a visual story. The caption adds context and personality rather than describing the image. Slightly more personal in tone; questions invite comments. |
| **Twitter/X** | Conversational, witty, timely | Real-time conversation. Short, sharp, easy to share. Threads carry longer stories, one complete thought per tweet. Witty, never at anyone's expense. |

## Where the strategy deck is superseded

The Unified Communication Strategy loosens three rules. **House voice wins.** Owner decision; not open for re-litigation.

| The deck says | AZMX house voice |
|---|---|
| 3–5 hashtags per post, and 5–10 on Instagram | **Three maximum, at the end.** Every channel, every brand. |
| Emojis are encouraged on Instagram and Twitter/X for tone and scannability | **No emojis** in copy: headlines, body, captions, subject lines, social posts. Every channel, every brand. The one exception is the email design system's section-header and digest chip glyph (C03, C04, C07, C16), which is a visual chip, not copy. |
| Every post must include a clear call to action | **No mandatory CTA.** Where a next step exists, make it obvious. Where it does not, do not manufacture one. |

An agent reading this skill must never conclude that 5–10 Instagram hashtags, emojis in copy, or a bolted-on CTA are permitted on AZMX work. The email chip glyph is the single exception, and it is a component, not a sentence.

## Pre-publish checklist

Run every piece through this before it goes live.

1. **On-brand?** Does it match the Four Dimensions above, or the correct sub-brand's profile in `sub-brand-voices.md`?
2. **Clear and concise?** Can it be said in fewer words?
3. **Valuable?** Does it pass the "what's in it for me?" test for the named audience?
4. **Purposeful?** Is the one takeaway obvious, and is the next step obvious to a reader who wants one? (The deck asks "does it have a CTA?" — AZMX asks this instead.)
5. **Right for the platform?** Is the tone tuned for LinkedIn, Instagram, or Twitter/X?
6. **Proofread, and mechanics clean?** Typos, grammar, the correct brand name spelled correctly. No em-dash defaults, no triads, no banned intensifiers, no emojis in the copy, no more than three hashtags. (The email design system's section-header and digest chip glyph is the one permitted exception; it is a visual chip, not copy.)

## Quick self-check before shipping copy

1. Could a competitor paste this over their logo? If yes, it is too generic; sharpen it.
2. Read it aloud. Anything you would not say to a client's face, rewrite.
3. Delete the first sentence if the second one is stronger without it. It usually is.
