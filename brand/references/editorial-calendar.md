# AZMX Editorial Calendar

The shape of a standard AZM X communication month: what runs on which channel, in what theme, at what time, at what volume. Read this when planning content, scheduling posts, or asked "what should we publish this week". Source: the AZM X Unified Communication Strategy channel plans.

This is a **repeating template, not a dated schedule.** The deck ships week-by-week grids naming every brand, platform, and send time across four channel plans. Those grids are an operations artifact for whoever holds the scheduling tool, and they are not reproduced here. What is reproduced is everything an agent needs to place a new piece of content correctly: the weekly themes, the cadence, the times, the CTA progression, and the initiative lists.

**House voice overrides the deck on social mechanics.** The strategy deck sets a three-tier hashtag approach and asks for 3–5 hashtags per post; its Instagram and Twitter/X guidance permits 5–10 hashtags and encourages emojis. **All of that is superseded.** AZMX house voice is three hashtags maximum, placed at the end, and no emojis in copy, on every channel and every brand. The one carve-out is the email design system's section-header and digest chip glyph, which is a visual chip rather than copy. See `voice-and-tone.md`.

## The operating month

| Week | Social Media | Digital Properties | Content & Email | Search Marketing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Provide Value | Practitioners | Practitioner Focus | Foundational Research & Strategy |
| **2** | Showcase Results & Engage Community | Business Value | Business Focus | Content Optimization & Campaign Launch |
| **3** | Deepen Expertise | Community | Community Focus | In-Flight Optimization & Authority Building |
| **4** | Drive Product Adoption & Build Brand | Strategic Partnership | Decision-Maker Focus | Reporting & Next-Cycle Planning |

The four columns are deliberately in step. Week 1 speaks to practitioners, week 2 to the business case, week 3 to the community, week 4 to the decision-maker and the next cycle. A new piece of content inherits the theme of the week it lands in.

Direct Sales runs on a quarterly asset cycle, Internal Initiatives on an initiative-by-initiative cycle, and Live & Environmental on a quarterly rhythm. Those three do not follow the weekly grid.

## Social Media

Themes as above. Guiding principles, compressed:

- **Engagement.** The schedule covers posting only. Allocate 30–60 minutes a day to replying to comments, handling mentions, and joining industry conversations.
- **Visual consistency.** Every graphic and thumbnail follows its own brand's guidelines. AZM X work follows this skill; sub-brand work needs its own visual system confirmed (see `sub-brand-voices.md`).
- **Hashtags.** The deck's three tiers still describe *which* tags to reach for: broad (`#DigitalTransformation`, `#ProductInnovation`), niche (`#ResearchTools`, `#UXDesignTools`), branded (`#AZMX`, `#MadeWithAZMX`, `#Majarah`). The count is house voice: **three maximum, at the end.**
- **Flexibility.** The calendar is structure, not a cage. Reserve capacity for trending topics and breaking news.

## Digital Properties

Four websites, four jobs. Every property gets reviewed and improved every week so none goes static.

**Flag: there is no Clix property.** The deck names four websites — AZM X, Colab, Majarah, Anatomi — and Clix is not one of them, yet the cadence recap gives Clix one blog post and one email campaign a month with nowhere to host them. Either a Clix property exists and is missing from the channel plan, or those two Clix items publish somewhere else. Confirm before planning Clix content.

- **Week 1 Practitioners** — publish practitioner-focused blog posts, ship a feature tutorial, refresh a gallery, run a technical link check.
- **Week 2 Business Value** — publish a B2B case study, add a customer logo, clarify "for teams" pricing, push the "Save the Date" update to the Majarah homepage, launch a homepage CTA A/B test.
- **Week 3 Community** — interview a Majarah speaker on the blog, cross-promote Majarah from the product sites, go live with the event schedule, refresh a testimonial.
- **Week 4 Strategic Partnership** — feature the case study on the homepage, update meta descriptions, run a keyword ranking check, add the ticket-urgency banner, implement the winning A/B variant.

The three Majarah beats are one event funnel, in order: "Save the Date" in week 2 builds awareness, the live event schedule in week 3 opens registration, the ticket-urgency banner in week 4 closes it. Dropping the first beat leaves registration with nothing in front of it.

Principles: audience-first (never dilute a property's single job) · content fuels the role, and fresh content also signals activity to search engines, which is why every property is touched weekly rather than only when there is news · every update leads to a clear next step · fast, accessible, discoverable.

## Content & Email

The blog is the magnet, email is the engine. Blog posts publish Sunday at 9:00 AM; email campaigns send Tuesday and Thursday at 10:30 AM.

Friction is a per-surface, within-week mechanic, not a monthly ramp. The Sunday blog post is the low-friction Lead Gen (Download) entry point in all four weeks without exception; escalation happens only on the Tuesday and Thursday emails, and it resets every week. Week 2 stays flat on Lead Gen throughout, and week 4 hits Conversion (Act) on Tuesday and then comes back down to Lead Gen on Thursday, so nothing escalates monotonically across the month:

| Week | Theme | CTA progression |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Practitioner Focus | Lead Gen (Download) → Onboarding (Engage) → Engagement (Engage) |
| 2 | Business Focus | Lead Gen throughout |
| 3 | Community Focus | Lead Gen (Download) → Onboarding (Engage) → **Conversion (Act)** |
| 4 | Decision-Maker Focus | Lead Gen (Download) → **Conversion (Act)** → Lead Gen (Download) |

Principles: every piece maps to one named persona · solve, do not sell · design the hand-off from blog to email deliberately · within a week, CTAs progress from low-friction ("Download a guide") to high-friction ("Contact us") · one ecosystem, five voices.

## Search Marketing

A four-week optimisation cycle. SEO builds long-term organic authority; PPC buys immediate high-intent leads and returns fast market insight that feeds SEO.

| Component | 8 initiatives per month |
|---|---|
| **SEO** | Keyword Research · Technical Audit · Content Optimization · On-Page SEO · Link Building · YouTube SEO · Rank Tracking · Competitor Analysis |
| **PPC** | Campaign Planning · Performance Review · Campaign Launch · Ad Copy A/B Test · Budget Review · Search Query Analysis · Reporting · Next Month Planning |

Search work runs on the content calendar's named outputs, in a fixed lag rather than as a parallel track: week 2's on-page SEO targets the new blog post, and week 3's link building targets the B2B case study published in Digital Properties week 2. If the content slips, the search task slips with it.

Named tools: Ahrefs, SEMrush, Google Ads and Ads Planner, LinkedIn Ads, Google Analytics, Google Data Studio, YouTube Studio, Pitchbox, Google Optimize.

Metrics: SEO reports organic traffic, keyword rankings, inbound leads. PPC reports cost per lead and return on ad spend.

Principles: own the answer to the query, not the keyword · dual strategy, SEO long-term and PPC immediate · target intent, so PPC budget goes to keywords signalling that a user is buying now, and the goal is qualified leads rather than clicks · every ad and result lands on a page that matches the query.

## Direct Sales & Client Success

A quarterly asset-management system: one category of sales material reviewed and improved each week.

| Asset type | Standing initiatives | Brands |
|---|---|---|
| Company Profile & Templates | Refresh performance stats and team bios · build a standard one-pager per brand · run a Business Development feedback session | All brands |
| Sales & Demo Presentations | Update master demo decks for new features and UI · build a B2G-specific demo deck (security and compliance) · refine the demo script against the top prospect questions | All brands · Colab, Anatomi · Colab, Clix |
| Proposals & SOWs | Add a high-impact case study slide to the master proposal · standardise pricing and scope language across SOWs · build a library of pre-approved RFP answers | AZM X |
| Project Reports & Deliverables | Standard monthly client progress report template · updated project kick-off deck template · Client Success feedback session | AZM X |

Principles: one voice from first deck to final report · frame around the client's pain and the value delivered · no jargon, explicit scope and timelines · a regular review cycle so no one ships stale statistics.

## Live & Environmental

Seasonal by nature, so it runs on a quarterly framework rather than a monthly template. Three tiers per quarter:

| Tier | Channel | Frequency per quarter | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | Public & Community Events | ~2 major initiatives | Long-planned external moments that set the quarter's strategic focus. This is where Majarah lives, alongside client summits and flagship conferences. |
| **Rhythm** | Team & Culture Events | ~3 initiatives | Internal engagement that builds culture and turns the team into advocates: annual gatherings, rituals, knowledge-sharing, seasonal celebrations. |
| **Pulse** | Workplace Communications | 12–13 weekly updates | Always-on ambient comms across in-office screens, laptop backgrounds, and Slack. |

Principles: a brand is felt, not just seen · inside out, so internal experience is built first · design for connection between people, not just between brand and audience · obsess over details, from event check-in to the motion on an office screen.

Metrics: internal tiers report employee engagement scores, participation rates, pulse survey feedback, message recall, and uptake on promoted initiatives. External events report registrations and attendance, attendee NPS, social mentions, and leads generated.
