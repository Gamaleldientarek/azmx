The operating system for how AZM X communicates: channels, roles, workflows, rituals, and the standard the work is held to. Read this before planning a campaign, briefing creative, or deciding who owns a deliverable. Source: the AZM X Unified Communication Strategy, the company's official 126-page deck.

## Purpose

Establish one consistent, measurable approach to how AZM X communicates internally and externally. AZM X is a leading Saudi digital consultancy; its philosophy line is **"Designing the Future of Experience."**

Two visions run in parallel and depend on each other:

| | Vision |
|---|---|
| **Internal** | A culture of alignment, transparency, motivation, and engagement. |
| **External** | Brand positioning built on client trust, visually compelling work, and clear thought leadership. |

## The challenge

Six failures the strategy exists to fix.

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Siloed team updates | Inconsistent market messaging |
| Unclear strategic direction | Lack of brand visibility |
| Weak employee engagement | Fragmented brand voice |

**The opportunity:** one voice, built to drive growth from the inside out.

## The five guiding principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Clarity over complexity** | Simple, direct, transparent messaging. No jargon. |
| **Consistency is key** | One brand, one voice, everywhere. |
| **Value-driven content** | Every piece educates, informs, or inspires. If it does none of the three, cut it. |
| **Dialogue, not monologue** | Listen and engage. Build community rather than broadcast at it. |
| **Creativity as a differentiator** | Design and storytelling are the competitive edge, not decoration on top of one. |

## The POEM model

Paid, Owned, and Earned each do one job, in sequence. Judge a channel by the job it holds, not by its volume.

| | Definition | Role | Channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Placement AZM X pays for, to reach a named audience. | Reach & Lead Generation | LinkedIn Ads, Google Ads, event sponsorships, influencer marketing |
| **Owned** | Channels and platforms AZM X controls directly. | Nurture & Conversion | Websites, blog, email, proposals, reports |
| **Earned** | Third parties talking about AZM X. Digital word of mouth. | Credibility & Trust | Press mentions, customer reviews, social shares, guest articles and talks |

Paid buys attention, Owned converts it, Earned is what makes either credible. Earned cannot be bought or scheduled; it is produced by work good enough to be talked about.

## Governance

### RACI definitions

| Letter | Role |
|---|---|
| **R — Responsible** | Does the work |
| **A — Accountable** | Owns the outcome |
| **C — Consulted** | Provides input |
| **I — Informed** | Is kept up to date |

**Two defects in the printed matrix.** Read them the way the per-role accountabilities below read them, because those are unambiguous:

1. *Overall Content & Editorial Strategy* shows two Accountables. Communication owns it. Creative is Consulted.
2. *Overall Visual & Creative Direction* shows Creative as Accountable and Design as Responsible. It is the reverse: the Head of Design owns final visual direction, and Creative plus Creative Designer execute.

Role naming also drifts between slides. Treat *Content Writer* and *Content Creator* as one role, *Design* as *Head of Design*, and *BD* as *Business Development*.

## Workflows

The goal is to move from reactive, ad-hoc communication to a proactive, structured system.

### External content workflow

For significant pieces: blog posts, case studies, website content. Anything needing multiple stakeholders and real polish.

| Stage | When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Ideation & Briefing** | Monthly and quarterly | Communication, Marketing, relevant stakeholders including Business Development | An idea is selected against quarterly themes and performance data. Communication writes a formal Content Brief naming the target persona, key message, channel, SEO keywords, and desired CTA. |
| **02 Content Creation & Review** | Per-project timeline | Content Creator, Communication, Marketing | The Content Creator writes the full draft. Communication reviews it for accuracy and fact, and that review is not optional. |
| **03 Creative Production** | After copy approval | Creative Lead, Creative Designer | Approved copy passes to creative as one complete request. They produce every visual: header images, social graphics, infographics. |
| **04 Delivery & Reports** | Per-project timeline | Communication, Marketing | Communication gives final brand and strategic sign-off. Marketing publishes on the designated platform and schedules promotion. |

Copy is approved before design starts. One complete request, not a trickle of partial briefs.

### Agile social media workflow

Plans a whole month in advance so the month itself is spent executing and engaging.

| Stage | When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Monthly Scoping & Briefing** | During the Monthly Content Planning Meeting, third week of the current month | Communication, Digital Marketing | Digital Marketing takes the approved themes and major pieces, then outlines the full social cadence including promotional posts and daily engagement content. |
| **02 Full-Month Content Creation** | Last week of the current month | Digital Marketing, Content Creator, Creative Designer | Copy is drafted for every planned post, then submitted to the Creative Designer as one comprehensive visual brief. |
| **03 Monthly Batch Review & Approval** | By the last business day of the current month | Communication | Communication reviews the entire month of copy and visuals in one consolidated batch and approves the full schedule. |
| **04 Scheduling & Real-Time Engagement** | First business day of the new month, then daily | Digital Marketing | Every approved post is scheduled in the social management tool. Focus shifts to monitoring channels, posting timely content that could not be planned, and tracking performance. |

### The other four channel workflows

| Channel | Workflow shape |
|---|---|
| Internal Initiatives | Initiative-based, running off an annual culture calendar. HR and Communication plan logistics, draft the internal comms, get stakeholder approval, then execute and announce. |
| Digital Properties | Monthly cycle of continuous improvement. Major thematic updates blended with small optimisation tasks, so every site is reviewed weekly and never goes static. |
| Search Marketing | Structured four-week optimisation cycle. |
| Direct Sales & Client Success | Quarterly asset-management system, reviewing one major category of sales material each week. |

### The three operating rituals

| Ritual | When | Who attends | Agenda |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekly Content Huddle** | Every Sunday, 30 minutes | Communication, Marketing, Digital Marketing, Creative | Review last week's key metrics · confirm this week's content and campaign priorities · surface and clear immediate roadblocks |
| **Monthly Content Planning Meeting** | Third week of every month, 60 minutes | The huddle team plus Content Creator and a Business Development representative | Review the month's performance against goals · finalise next month's content themes, major blog posts, and campaign initiatives |
| **Quarterly Strategy Review** | Last week of the quarter, 90 minutes | All monthly attendees plus Head of Design and other department leads | Review the quarter against high-level goals such as lead generation and brand awareness · set strategic themes and priorities for the next quarter |

## Creative evaluation

The approval process is held to the same standard as the work. Every piece of creative gets scored on one scale.

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Destructive | Hurts AZMX, the brand, or society. Never produce this. |
| 2 | Un-noticed | Pedestrian work that gets ignored. Wasted money and effort. |
| 3 | Decent | A decent idea, decently made, that does not build the brand. Not enough. |
| 4 | Creative | Gets buzz, contributes nothing to long-term growth. A missed opportunity. |
| 5 | **Strategically Creative** | **The target.** Gets the brand and the audience right, builds equity and growth, earns media attention. |

Aim at 5 every time. The riskiest idea is the one nobody notices; be brave rather than safe.

### Four critique standards

| Standard | The question to ask |
|---|---|
| **Always start with the brief** | Does this work meet the objectives of the brief? |
| **Champion the audience** | Not "do I like it" but "does this respond to the insight we have about our audience?" |
| **Use our shared language** | Discuss the composition, the brand voice, or the idea. Name the thing, so feedback is not taste. |
| **Assess for strategic creativity** | Is it fresh? Will it surprise and engage people? Will it differentiate the brand? |

### Four feedback standards

| Standard | What it commits you to |
|---|---|
| **We are one team** | Teammates, not vendors and clients. Everyone in the room cares about the brand. |
| **We define the problem, not the solution** | State the issue or the strategic gap. Do not prescribe the fix; that is the expert's job. |
| **We critique through the lens of the audience** | Evaluate against the audience and the strategy, not personal taste. |
| **We will be honest and diplomatic** | Direct and honest, delivered with respect. Both halves, always. |

## Creative mindsets

Three shared mindsets so non-specialists can brief and critique well. Creativity belongs to everyone: understand the why, collaborate better, and give objective feedback instead of opinion.

**C.H.O.I.C.E. thinking** — the filter every idea passes through:

| | | Question |
|---|---|---|
| C | Context | How does this fit into people's daily lives and routines? |
| H | Humanistic | Is it decent, ethical, and additive to the world? |
| O | Observation | What insight is hiding in people's actual behaviour? |
| I | Interesting | Is it appealing enough to stop someone and make them participate? |
| C | Craft | Great idea, great execution. Skillful making, not just a clever thought. |
| E | Empathy | Does it identify with what people need and want? |

**The creative process:**

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **01 Preparation** | Research until an insight appears. Ask the stupid questions. |
| **02 Initial Ideation** | Synthesise, and generate concepts from the insight. |
| **03 Incubation** | Step away and let it breathe. |
| **04 Illumination** | Generate several substantially different ideas. |
| **05 Verification** | Test the final idea for clear benefit, freshness, and fit with the brand narrative. |

**The copywriting mindset:**

| Mindset | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Lead with empathy** | Write until the audience thinks "you know me". |
| **Offer a benefit** | Every reader is asking what is in it for them. |
| **Tell stories** | Stories rather than pitches. |

| Step | The copywriting process |
|---|---|
| **01** | Define the intent: seduce, compel, inform, create desire, or build community. |
| **02** | Find a distinctive voice, belonging to the brand rather than the writer. |
| **03** | Write with clarity: short sentences, active voice, no adjective padding. |
| **04** | Show rather than tell. |
| **05** | Rewrite. There is no good writing, only good rewriting. |

**The design mindset:**

| Mindset | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Design influences decisions** | It shapes what people choose, not only how the work looks. |
| **Design is visual communication** | Not decoration applied on top. |
| **Typography is a voice** | Selecting type is a strategic decision. |

| Step | The design process |
|---|---|
| **01** | Start with why rather than what, so appearance is decided last. |
| **02** | Establish visual hierarchy. |
| **03** | Compose for balance. |
| **04** | Create unity and rhythm through repeated elements. |
| **05** | Choose type with intent. |

## Briefing

A brief is the most important step in the process, not an administrative one. Write briefs worth framing.

| Checkpoint | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **01 A clear objective** | Define what success looks like before writing anything else. |
| **02 A clear insight** | A genuine human truth about the audience's behaviour or perception, that the creative can connect to. Do the work to find it. |
| **03 A three-sentence story** | The strategy, stated in three lines. See below. |
| **04 Honest criteria** | How the creative will be executed and how its success will be judged, stated at the start so nobody is surprised at the end. |

**The three-sentence story:**

> The client/audience wants ______.
> The obstacle to getting what the client/audience wants is ______.
> Our brand can facilitate what the client/audience wants because ______.

If those three sentences will not close cleanly, the strategy is not ready and no amount of craft will save the output.

### The Idea, or North Star

Every piece of work starts from a defined Idea: the central premise that keeps every execution conceptually sound and connects each ad, post, and email into one story.

| | |
|---|---|
| **It provides cohesion** | The thread that makes a social video and a website banner ladder up to the same message even when they look nothing alike. |
| **It must be flexible** | Not a rigid rule. It has to survive being expressed as a video, a static image, and an interactive experience. |
| **It drives every decision** | Art direction, copy, visual style, and palette are all chosen because they serve the Idea and the brand's purpose. |

## Pain points and solutions

| Pain point | The fix |
|---|---|
| **Inconsistent brand messaging** — brands and departments communicate differently, fragmenting identity and confusing customers | A tone-of-voice guide per brand holds personality steady; a unified Content Brief means every initiative starts from a clear, on-brand message |
| **Information silos and lack of alignment** — teams do not know what other teams are communicating, so opportunities are missed and messages collide; employees feel disconnected from direction | A master editorial calendar as the single source of truth, plus the operating rituals, starting with the Weekly Content Huddle |
| **Inefficient content creation** — creation, review, and approval are chaotic and slow; the wheel gets reinvented and quality varies | Defined workflows for external content, social, and internal comms, plus master templates for sales and success material |
| **Difficulty measuring ROI** — hard to connect communication to business goals, hard to defend the budget | Every channel plan carries defined metrics, and a performance dashboard tracks them for data-driven decisions |

## Change management

A strategy is worth only its adoption. Four phases.

| Phase | Approach | Goal | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Foundation** | Align & Prepare | Build core infrastructure and secure leadership alignment before the company-wide launch | Final approval on the strategy · form the Communications Council · build the master editorial calendar, the initial performance dashboard, and the first essential templates |
| **02 Launch** | Announce & Educate | Introduce the strategy company-wide, explain the why, and train | Unveil it at an All-Hands · run short targeted training on the new workflows, tools, and templates · put every guide and template on the intranet |
| **03 First 30–90 Days** | Implement & Pilot | Put it into practice, learn from real execution, adjust fast | Run the first month's cadence as a pilot · the Communications Council meets weekly through this phase to clear roadblocks · gather feedback from every team |
| **04 New Standard** | Reinforce & Optimize | Embed the system in daily operations and build continuous improvement | Share the performance dashboard in monthly and quarterly meetings · publicly recognise teams who adopt the processes well · use the data to sharpen next quarter's strategy |
